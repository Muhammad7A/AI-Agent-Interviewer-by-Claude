# The Consultant Operating Model — Groundwork

> The human operating system around the product. Not screens, not code — how a
> consultant, analyst, or transformation lead actually uses Groundwork end-to-end to
> deliver value. Designed for *real consulting behavior*, not fantasy product
> behavior. The test it must pass: after one engagement, the consultant says *"that
> saved me a week, I trusted the output, and I looked smarter in the room."*

---

## 1. The consultant's job-to-be-done (the reframe)

A discovery consultant's job was never "gather and read everything." It is: **form a
defensible point of view about how the organization actually works, evidence it,
pressure-test it, and use it to move an executive to a decision.**

Groundwork **collapses the labor and amplifies the judgment.** It does the gathering
(scheduling, interviewing, note-taking, first-pass synthesis) so the consultant's
hours move to the 20% that only a human can do — deciding what's true, what matters,
what's political, and what story drives action.

**The effort curve — where the week goes.** (~15-interview engagement, illustrative.)

| Phase | Manual today | With Groundwork | What changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setup & framing | ~6h | ~6h | unchanged — it's judgment |
| Scheduling & interviewing | ~25h | ~2h (monitor) | **collapsed** |
| Note-taking & first synthesis | ~20h | ~0 (AI drafts) | **collapsed** |
| Judging findings / contradictions / priority | ~10h | ~12h | **amplified** — more time on the hard part |
| Report & presentation | ~15h | ~6h | assisted |
| **Total** | **~76h** | **~26h** | **~65% less, spent on higher-leverage work** |

The point is not just speed. It is that **the time that remains is the time that makes
the consultant valuable.** That is why they feel *smarter, not replaced.*

## 2. Operating principles (the constitution of the workflow)

1. **AI proposes, the consultant owns the truth.** Nothing AI-generated reaches the
   client unless the consultant validates it. This is a safety property, not a slogan.
2. **Editing by exception.** The consultant does not review everything equally. The
   system triages by confidence × corroboration × stakes; strong/corroborated findings
   get a glance and a batch-accept, weak/contested/sensitive ones get deep judgment.
   Attention is the scarce resource; the workflow allocates it.
3. **Never buried in transcript.** Raw transcript is *available on one click*, never
   *required*. The default surface is findings, not text.
4. **Evidence always one click away.** Every finding drills to the verbatim quote and
   the full interview. Trust is inspectable, never asked-for.
5. **No busywork to feel advanced.** If a step doesn't change a decision or a
   deliverable, it doesn't exist.
6. **Automate gathering and organizing; the human owns framing, judging, persuading.**
   That is the dividing line the whole model turns on (§8).

**Three global rules (stated once, true at every stage):**
- *If the consultant ignores an AI suggestion* → the item stays **Proposed** and is
  **excluded from the deliverable.** Ignoring is always safe.
- *If the consultant disagrees with the AI* → they **reject or edit**; the consultant
  is the final authority, and the disagreement is captured as a **learning signal**
  (the engine was wrong/mis-ranked here).
- *What's saved for learning at every stage* → every accept/edit/reject/merge, the
  edit distance, usefulness ratings, and later the decision-outcome — logged to the
  four-layer memory, near-zero extra effort.

## 3. The 13-stage workflow

Each stage: **goal · actions · AI assist · human owns · in → out · key decision ·
failure mode.** (Override/ignore/learning behavior follows the global rules above
unless noted.)

**Stage 1 · Pre-engagement setup.** *Goal:* turn the client's problem into a sharp
learning objective + starting hypotheses. *Actions:* consultant writes the focus
("where does finance lose time; what's automatable?"). *AI assist:* proposes a focus
frame + candidate hypotheses from the stated problem. *Human owns:* the framing — this
seeds the whole interview engine and is pure judgment. *In:* client brief. *Out:*
engagement objective + hypotheses. *Decision:* what question are we really answering?
*Failure:* a vague focus → shallow interviews. **Most consultant-heavy step; automate
the least.**

**Stage 2 · Company / project setup.** *Goal:* configure scope, consent, timeline.
*Actions:* enter client contact, dates, confidentiality terms. *AI assist:* generates
the employee-facing consent/comms package. *Human owns:* the legal/trust framing
(works council, GDPR, anonymity promise). *In:* engagement terms. *Out:* configured
engagement + comms kit. *Decision:* what's shared with whom (the trust architecture:
consultant sees words, employer sees anonymized aggregates). *Failure:* legal/consent
gap kills the pilot before interview one. **Mostly manual admin; AI drafts comms.**

**Stage 3 · Interview target selection.** *Goal:* pick a sample that yields diverse,
independent standpoints. *Actions:* consultant pastes/curates the invite list with role
labels. *AI assist:* suggests a sample frame — which roles/functions to cover for
standpoint diversity (least-correlated vantages), flags gaps ("no one from AP"). *Human
owns:* the final list — they know the politics the AI can't see. *In:* org context.
*Out:* invite list. *Decision:* who, and how many. *Failure:* a skewed sample → a
biased picture that looks confident. *Ignore AI:* fine — their list stands.

**Stage 4 · Interview launch.** *Goal:* get people to actually participate. *Actions:*
send invites; secure a client-contact endorsement (the real participation driver).
*AI assist:* sends magic-link invites; drafts the framing message. *Human owns:* the
tone and the internal sponsorship. *In:* list. *Out:* interviews in flight. *Decision:*
timing, framing, who endorses. *Failure:* ignored invites → low N → thin data. **Fully
automatable except the human sponsorship.**

**Stage 5 · Live monitoring.** *Goal:* know if the engagement is on track *without*
reading anything. *Actions:* glance at a light progress view. *AI assist:* completion
rate, early emerging themes, low-engagement flags, "rich signal on X, thin on Y —
consider adding 2 interviews." *Human owns:* whether to nudge, extend, or add people.
*In:* live interviews. *Out:* a go/adjust decision. *Decision:* is N and signal
sufficient? *Failure:* letting a low-completion engagement run to a weak conclusion.
**The one place a small dashboard earns its keep. Consultant does NOT read transcripts
here.**

**Stage 6 · Interview review (deliberately minimized).** *Goal:* calibrate trust and
spot-check — not read everything. *Actions:* skim 2-3 flagged interviews (richest,
most contradictory). *AI assist:* per-interview 3-line summary + candor/signal flag +
jump-to-quote. *Human owns:* sampling judgment. *In:* transcripts. *Out:* calibration.
*Decision:* do I trust this batch enough to work from the findings? *Failure:* the trap
of reading all 15 (the exact behavior the product exists to eliminate). **If the
consultant is reading every transcript, the workflow has failed.**

**Stage 7 · Insight review (the core).** *Goal:* decide what's true. *Actions:* work
the ranked findings top-down; **batch-accept** strong+corroborated, **deep-review**
weak/contested/high-stakes. *AI assist:* candidate findings ranked, each with evidence
quotes, **N-of-M** corroboration, a confidence band, and a one-line "why it matters."
*Human owns:* accept / edit / reject / merge — the truth. *In:* candidate findings.
*Out:* a validated set. *Decision:* real or noise? *Failure:* rubber-stamping (accept
all) or nihilism (reject all). *Ignore:* the finding stays Proposed, out of the report.
*Disagree:* reject/edit; logged as the engine being wrong. **This is where the
consultant spends the most judgment, and it should feel fast — triage, not slog.**

**Stage 8 · Contradiction review.** *Goal:* adjudicate the tensions — where the real
insight hides. *Actions:* review each flagged contradiction with both sides' evidence.
*AI assist:* surfaces contradictions classified as *conflict* (someone's wrong),
*variation* (a finding: "the process is non-standardized"), or *complementary* (both
true), with quotes from each side. *Human owns:* the adjudication — is this an error to
resolve, a genuine variation to report, or two valid vantages? This is the most
judgment-dense stage and the one where consultant expertise most out-performs the AI.
*In:* contradictions. *Out:* resolved findings (incl. "non-standardized" as a finding).
*Decision:* error vs finding vs noise. *Failure:* flattening real variation into false
consensus, or over-weighting one dissenter.

**Stage 9 · Opportunity prioritization.** *Goal:* rank what to recommend. *Actions:*
re-rank the AI's draft priority against client reality. *AI assist:* proposes a ranking
(impact × feasibility, grouped by theme). *Human owns:* the re-rank using constraints
the AI can't see — budget, politics, strategy, the CEO's pet project. *In:* validated
findings. *Out:* a prioritized shortlist. *Decision:* what makes the cut and in what
order. *Failure:* shipping the AI's internal ranking that ignores the client's actual
constraints. *Disagree:* the consultant's ranking always wins; the delta is learning.

**Stage 10 · Validation & editing (make it mine).** *Goal:* make every finding
defensible, actionable, and in the consultant's voice. *Actions:* edit wording, adjust
confidence, cut horoscope findings, sharpen the "so what." *AI assist:* drafts the
prose and suggests confidence labels. *Human owns:* voice, accuracy, actionability,
and how it lands on the client's ears. *In:* validated set. *Out:* client-ready
findings. *Decision:* is each finding true, specific, and defensible? *Saved:* edit
distance (how much they changed the AI = a direct synthesis-quality signal).

**Stage 11 · Final report generation.** *Goal:* produce the deliverable. *Actions:*
review the auto-assembled report; add strategic framing. *AI assist:* assembles a
structured, evidence-backed report from validated items + the honesty box (*confident
enough to act on* vs *needs more discovery*). *Human owns:* the narrative arc and the
executive framing. *In:* validated findings. *Out:* the report (PDF / deck / link).
*Decision:* does this tell a true, actionable story? *Failure:* an overconfident report
on thin evidence — the honesty box exists to prevent it. **The consultant owns this as
their work.**

**Stage 12 · Client presentation.** *Goal:* move the executive to a decision. *Actions:*
present; handle pushback. *AI assist:* behind the scenes — instant evidence backup
(quotes, N-of-M) when a finding is challenged. *Human owns:* reading the room, the
story, the credibility. *In:* the report. *Out:* a decision (or the next step).
*Decision:* how hard to push each recommendation. *Failure:* being unable to defend a
finding live (mitigated by having inspected evidence in Stages 7-8). **The consultant
is the face; Groundwork is the notebook. They say "we interviewed 20 people and here's what
we learned," never "the AI found this."**

**Stage 13 · Post-engagement learning.** *Goal:* capture what worked, at near-zero
effort. *Actions:* a 60-second wrap: which findings the client acted on, a usefulness
rating, a time-saved estimate. *AI assist:* pre-fills what it can; later tracks decision
outcomes over months. *Human owns:* the honest retrospective. *In:* the engagement.
*Out:* the learning signal (validation labels + outcomes → the four-layer memory).
*Decision:* would I run this again, and where did it fail me? *Failure:* the consultant
skips it (so it must be effortless) — this is where the moat compounds or doesn't.

## 4. Consultant surfaces, decisions, AI suggestions, override points

**Surfaces** (from the operating angle): Engagement setup · Sample builder · Live
monitor (the one dashboard) · Interview spot-check + transcript reader · **Findings
triage (the home base)** · Contradiction adjudicator · Priority ranker · Report editor
· Presentation evidence-backup · Learning wrap.

**Key decisions the human owns:** the engagement *question* (1) · the *sample* (3) · the
*trust/consent* framing (2) · *what's true* (7) · *error vs variation* (8) · the
*priority* against client reality (9) · the *narrative* (11) · *how to sell it* (12).

**AI suggestions by stage:** focus/hypotheses (1) · consent comms (2) · sample frame +
gaps (3) · invite copy (4) · progress + emerging themes + "add interviews" (5) · per-
interview summaries + flags (6) · ranked findings + evidence + N-of-M + confidence (7) ·
classified contradictions (8) · draft priority ranking (9) · draft prose + confidence
labels (10) · assembled report + honesty box (11) · live evidence backup (12) · pre-
filled retro (13).

**Override points (every proposal is overridable):** re-frame the focus · change the
sample · accept/edit/reject/merge any finding · re-classify any contradiction · re-rank
any priority · rewrite any prose · relabel any confidence · exclude anything from the
report. **There is no AI output the consultant cannot change, and none reaches the
client unvalidated.**

## 5. Trust model (earned, not assumed)

Trust is built through four mechanisms, progressively across the engagement:
- **Transparency** — every finding drills to the verbatim quote; nothing is a black box.
- **Calibration** — confidence is honest (High/Med/Low, fragile flags, the honesty box);
  the system that says "low confidence" and is right earns more trust than one always
  sure.
- **Control** — accept/edit/reject/merge on everything; the human is always final.
- **Provenance** — every item is visibly *AI-Proposed* until *Consultant-Validated*.

Trust *ratchets*: the consultant spot-checks heavily in the first engagement (Stage 6),
finds the evidence holds, and spot-checks less in the fifth. The workflow supports both
the skeptic (drill everything) and the veteran (batch-accept the strong) without forcing
either.

---

## 6. Learning model (the moat, captured at zero effort)

The consultant's ordinary actions *are* the training signal — no separate labeling:
- **Accept/reject/edit** → precision/recall signal on findings.
- **Edit distance** → synthesis fidelity (low edit = trusted draft).
- **Contradiction adjudications** → which patterns are real findings.
- **Priority re-ranks** → the gap between AI logic and client reality.
- **Usefulness + "would I have missed it?"** → decision-usefulness + novelty.
- **Decision outcomes** (months later) → the gold reward signal.
Everything flows into the four-layer memory (testimony / interpretation / validation /
outcome, never fused). The rule: **if capturing the signal costs the consultant effort,
they won't do it — so it must be a byproduct of work they already do.**

---

## 7. Handoff to executives

- The **consultant is the face; Groundwork is the notebook.** The exec never logs in and
  never hears "the AI decided."
- The **artifact** is the report + an evidence backup the consultant can summon live.
- The **anonymity architecture holds:** the exec gets anonymized, aggregated,
  N-of-M-backed findings ("7 of 9 in finance, independently") — credible *and* employee-
  protecting.
- The **honesty box** ("confident enough to act on / needs more discovery") is what makes
  an executive trust the confident parts — and it's the consultant's credibility
  insurance.
- The consultant's authority comes from having *inspected the evidence themselves*
  (Stages 7-8), so they can defend any finding without leaning on the tool in the room.

## 8. Where to trust automation vs intervene manually

| Trust the automation (gathering & organizing) | Intervene manually (framing, judging, persuading) |
|---|---|
| Conducting interviews | Framing the engagement question (1) |
| Transcription & summarization | Selecting the sample (3) |
| First-pass extraction & evidence-linking | Deciding what's true (7) |
| Ranking findings *as a draft* | Adjudicating contradictions (8) |
| Assembling the report | Prioritizing against client reality (9) |
| Sending invites, tracking progress | The narrative and the client room (11-12) |

**The dividing line:** automate the *labor of gathering and organizing*; the human owns
*framing, judging, and persuading* — and is always the final authority on truth.

---

## 9. Minimum viable consultant workflow (v1)

Thin and real: **frame focus → paste sample → send invites → (interviews run) →
triaged findings review with one-click evidence → validate (accept/edit/reject) →
export report.** Contradiction review and prioritization are *lightweight* passes over
the same findings screen. Everything else (sample optimization, live theme steering,
decision tracking) is deferred. The consultant can run a real engagement start-to-finish
in this loop.

## 10. Future-state consultant workflow

Add, in leverage order: AI sample optimization (standpoint coverage) · live signal
steering ("you're thin on the finance handoff — add 2 people") · cross-engagement
pattern reuse (the population prior sharpening cold-start) · decision-impact tracking
(closing the outcome loop) · a "challenge my hypothesis" mode where the consultant
argues with the AI's synthesis · multi-consultant collaboration on one engagement.

---

## 11. Final deliverable

**Must be MANUAL in v1** (the judgment): engagement framing · sample selection ·
consent/legal framing · contradiction adjudication · prioritization re-ranking · the
narrative · the client presentation. *If we automate these to look advanced, we ship a
worse deliverable and lose the consultant's trust.*

**Can be AUTOMATED in v1** (the labor): interviewing · transcription · first-pass
extraction · finding ranking (as a draft) · evidence-linking · report assembly · invite
sending · progress monitoring.

**Should stay FAKE/MANUAL behind the scenes in v1:** cross-interview fusion (one LLM
pass, looks automated) · confidence (corroboration count + consultant judgment, not a
model) · the "learning loop" (log the signal now; don't yet claim it improves the
engine) · sample suggestions (a heuristic) · decision-outcome tracking (a manual note).
*Never fake it in the copy — don't promise learning we haven't shipped.*

**Top 5 workflow risks**
1. **Rubber-stamping** — the consultant accepts all findings to save time, and ships the
   AI's errors as their own. (Mitigation: triage UI that forces attention on low-
   confidence/contested items.)
2. **The transcript trap** — the consultant reverts to reading everything, erasing the
   time saving. (Mitigation: findings-first default; make spot-checking feel sufficient.)
3. **Overconfident deliverable** — thin evidence presented as certainty; one burned exec
   kills the referral. (Mitigation: the honesty box, fragile flags, N-of-M.)
4. **Skipped learning capture** — no feedback, no compounding moat. (Mitigation: make it
   a 60-second byproduct, not a task.)
5. **Trust never forms** — the consultant distrusts the AI, rejects everything, and the
   tool merely saved transcription. (Mitigation: evidence drill-down + calibration in the
   first engagement; even the skeptic gets transcripts + a first draft.)

**Top 5 workflow opportunities**
1. **Attention as the product** — the real value is *where the consultant spends their
   judgment*; a great triage surface is the whole moat of the workflow.
2. **The contradiction stage is the differentiator** — no survey tool adjudicates
   espoused-vs-enacted; lean into it as the consultant's superpower.
3. **Evidence-on-demand makes consultants braver in the room** — they defend findings
   they couldn't have, faster.
4. **Zero-effort learning capture** turns every engagement into training data — the
   compounding advantage, if it's frictionless.
5. **"Feel smarter, not replaced"** is a wedge, not a slogan — a workflow that visibly
   moves the consultant up the value chain (from note-taker to decision-shaper) is one
   they evangelize internally, solving distribution.

---

**The operating system in one line:** Groundwork does the gathering; the consultant does the
judging and the persuading; the evidence is always one click away; nothing reaches the
client unvalidated; and every ordinary action the consultant takes quietly makes the
engine smarter.
